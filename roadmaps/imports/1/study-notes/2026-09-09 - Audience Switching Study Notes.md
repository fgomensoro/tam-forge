# Audience Switching Study Notes

## Corrected and validated note

### Concise rule

Keep the facts consistent while changing the emphasis, requested action, and level of detail for each audience.

### Plain-English explanation

Separate confirmed facts, proposed actions, and unknowns. Give a next-update commitment that does not depend on the fix being finished. Do not report mitigation, customer acceptance, or engineering progress without supporting evidence.

### Practical TAM example

- Integration engineer: explain the failure sequence, request persistence and reuse of the operation key, and specify a timeout-and-crash recovery test.
- VP Engineering: state customer impact, known mitigation status, priority request, ownership, blockers, and the next update.
- CFO: state duplicate financial exposure, refund and inventory status, reconciliation ownership, and the evidence required for closure.

### Boundary or common mistake

A technical fix is not evidence that a refund completed. Requested work is not completed work. A timeout alone does not prove a lost response or an ERP failure. Mixed-language attempts do not establish English-only delivery; written messages do not establish pronunciation or uninterrupted spoken performance.

### Card-ready Q/A

#### Card 1
Q: What does an integration engineer need from an incident handoff?
A: The observed failure sequence, a specific requested change, and a concrete verification test.
Why it matters for a TAM: The handoff should enable implementation and validation.

#### Card 2
Q: What should a VP Engineering update emphasize?
A: Customer impact, verified status, the priority decision or action needed, ownership, and the next update.
Why it matters for a TAM: Leadership needs enough information to allocate attention and resources.

#### Card 3
Q: What should a CFO update emphasize in a duplicate-order incident?
A: Financial exposure, reconciliation status, and proof that duplicate payments and inventory effects are corrected.
Why it matters for a TAM: Technical recovery must translate into verified business recovery.

#### Card 4
Q: How should you communicate when the resolution time is unknown?
A: State the uncertainty and commit to a specific next update without inventing a fix ETA.
Why it matters for a TAM: Predictable communication preserves trust without false certainty.
